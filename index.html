    Surfing Lessons

    🏄 Learn to surf near Uluwatu

    The Bukit has some of Bali's most famous waves, plus gentler beginner spots. Schools pick the beach each day depending on swell and tide. Beginner lessons usually run about 2 hours and cover ocean safety, paddling and standing up.

    Bali Surfing Camp · Padang Padang

    Established surf school near Uluwatu. You don't need to stay at the camp, and transport from the school to the break is included.

    Beginner lessons

    Soultide Surf School · Uluwatu

    A small, locally run school with a friendly, community feel.

    Visit website

    🌊 Beginner-friendly beaches nearby

    • "Baby Padang" at Padang Padang: a mellow wave popular for lessons
    • Balangan and Dreamland: sandy beaches often used by surf schools

    Uluwatu's main breaks are for experienced surfers only. Always go with an instructor if you're new.

    Know before you go

    Bali tips

    🛵Grab, Gojek & taxis
    • Grab is the easiest for visitors: English app, takes foreign credit cards, and has a pickup lounge at the airport arrivals area.
    • Gojek is often cheapest, especially GoRide motorbike hops. Foreign cards are sometimes refused, so keep cash as a backup.
    • “No pickup” zones: some village associations block app pickups in parts of Canggu, Ubud, Uluwatu and near some temples. Drop-offs are usually fine. If you see a “No Grab / No Gojek” sign, walk a little way out before booking, and don't argue with local drivers.
    • Bluebird taxis (blue cars, own app) are the trusted metered option. Make sure the meter is on.
    • Avoid drivers who approach you at tourist spots. They usually charge several times the fair price.
    • Tip small amounts in-app (Rp 5,000–10,000) for good service.
    • We'd rather you didn't rent a scooter for party nights. Book a car or share one.
    👗Dress codes

      Temples: cover shoulders and knees, and wear a sarong and sash (you can usually borrow or rent one at the entrance).

      Beach clubs: swimwear is fine by the pool, but bring a cover-up for the restaurant. Some clubs check bags at the door.

      🛂Arrival, visa & tourist levy

      🇦🇺 Australia · 🇳🇱 Netherlands · 🇮🇪 Ireland · 🇳🇿 New Zealand · 🇩🇪 Germany
      All five passports need a Visa on Arrival (VoA) for Bali. None of them can enter visa-free.

      CostIDR 500,000 per person (roughly AUD 50 / €30), children included Stay30 days, single entry ExtendOnce, for another 30 days (60 in total) PassportValid for at least 6 months from arrival Also needA return or onward flight booking

      Two ways to get it:

      • e-VoA online (recommended): apply and pay on the official evisa.imigrasi.go.id site before you fly, ideally at least 48 hours ahead. You skip the visa payment queue at the airport.
      • At the airport: pay at the VoA counter in arrivals, before immigration. Cards and cash are accepted.

      Your three arrival must-dos:

      • 1. Visa on Arrival (above)
      • 2. All Indonesia arrival card: free and mandatory, covering customs and health. Fill it in on allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id or the All Indonesia app within 3 days before you land, and keep the QR code on your phone.
      • 3. Bali tourist levy: IDR 150,000 per person, children included. Pay on the official Love Bali site or app and keep the QR voucher.
      ⚠️ Beware of scam sites. Official Indonesian government sites end in .go.id. Look-alike sites charge extra, and some never deliver a visa at all. The arrival card is always free.

      Rules can change before May 2027. Check your government's travel advice (e.g. Smartraveller for Australians) a few weeks before you fly.

      💸Money
      • Currency is the Indonesian rupiah (IDR). The zeros add up fast: Rp 100,000 is a small sum.
      • Cards work at most venues, sometimes with a ~3% surcharge. Carry small notes for drivers, warungs and tips.
      • Use ATMs inside banks or minimarts rather than street-side machines.
      🙏Local etiquette
      • Step around the small woven offerings (canang sari) you'll see on footpaths and doorsteps.
      • Give and receive things with your right hand.
      • Keep beachwear for the beach and pool.
      • Stay calm and smile. Raising your voice gets you nowhere in Bali.
      🩺Staying well
      • Drink bottled or filtered water only, including for brushing your teeth if you're sensitive.
      • The sun is strong near the equator. Bring reef-safe sunscreen, a hat and mosquito repellent.
      • Get travel insurance, and check it covers scooters if you plan to ride one.
      • Emergency number: 112.
